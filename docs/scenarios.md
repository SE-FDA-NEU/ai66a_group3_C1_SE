# User scenarios

These are complete user journeys in plain language. They do not refer to
screen names, route names, or button labels.

## Scenario 1 - A Frequent Explorer returns to a personal list

**Persona:** Frequent Explorer

1. The Frequent Explorer wants to find a new movie without spending a long
   time browsing.
2. They identify themselves to the service using the account they used
   previously.
3. The service restores their saved favourite genres for that account.
4. They receive a short list of movies related to those genres.
5. They read why each suggestion is relevant to their interests.
6. They narrow the current list to the genre they want to watch that evening.
7. They choose a movie and read its year, genres, and overview.
8. They explore similar alternatives and choose a more suitable movie.

## Scenario 2 - An Occasional Undecided Viewer begins safely

**Persona:** Occasional Undecided Viewer

1. The Occasional Undecided Viewer wants to watch a movie but does not know
   what to search for.
2. They explore a ranked selection of popular movies without providing
   personal data.
3. They decide to receive suggestions that they can keep for a later visit.
4. They create an account with an email and password, then identify
   themselves to the service.
5. They select several favourite genres.
6. They request suggestions based on those selections.
7. The catalogue contains no movie matching the current preferences.
8. They receive popular alternatives rather than reaching a dead end.
9. They adjust their genres, receive matching recommendations, and choose a
   movie to explore further.

## Scenario 3 - A Detail-Oriented Chooser compares similar options

**Persona:** Detail-Oriented Chooser

1. The Detail-Oriented Chooser wants a film that fits their interests but
   needs enough information to decide.
2. They identify themselves to the service and choose favourite genres.
3. They receive a relevant list and read the reason attached to one result.
4. They choose the movie and review its title, year, genres, and summary.
5. They decide that its summary is not right for the evening.
6. They look at films that share at least one genre and whose descriptions are
   more closely related to the original film.
7. They open one alternative and read its information.
8. They find a suitable option and decide whether to watch it.
